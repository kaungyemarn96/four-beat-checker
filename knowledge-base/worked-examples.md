# Worked examples

Loaded when you need to show what a fix looks like, or when the author disputes
a grade. Never on a first pass — the check should stand on its own evidence.

Current as of: August 2026.

---

## Example A — passes

Subject: why a content calendar stops working in month three.

| Beat | The line that carries it |
|---|---|
| Wrong question | "The question everyone asks is how many posts a month. It is the wrong number to be optimising." |
| Escape hatch | "What holds is a router: one fixed rule for where a new instruction goes, so nobody has to remember where anything lives." |
| Who uses it | "A fleet operator running twenty-eight of these across four teams cut their brief-to-draft time from nine days to two, on their own reporting." |
| Your turn | "Open whatever you use for briefs. Write down where a new instruction goes when it does not fit an existing rule. That single line is the router's first entry." |

**Verdict: ship.**

Note what makes beat 1 work: it names a number the reader is probably tracking
right now, then takes it away. A version that opened "content calendars are
broken" would be graded `weak` — nobody holds that as a belief worth correcting.

## Example B — fails on beat 3, and the fix

Same subject. Beats 1, 2 and 4 as above. Beat 3 reads:

> "Many organisations have found this approach delivers significant
> improvements in efficiency and consistency across their content operations."

**Grade: `missing`, not `weak`.** No actor, no number, nothing traceable. There
is no line to quote, only a claim that something worked somewhere.

**The one fix:** replace it with one named operator and one figure you can point
at. Not three — one, argued with rather than listed.

> "A fleet operator running twenty-eight of these across four teams cut
> brief-to-draft from nine days to two. The saving did not come from writing
> faster; it came from nobody having to ask where a rule lived."

The second sentence is what makes it a beat rather than a statistic. It says
what the number means, which is the job beat 3 exists to do.

## Example C — all four present, order wrong

A draft that opens with the router, explains it for four paragraphs, then says
"most teams think the answer is posting more often," then the proof, then the
action.

**Every beat is `present`. The verdict is still `restructure`.**

The escape hatch arrives before the reader has a reason to want it, so four
paragraphs are spent explaining a solution to a problem not yet stated. Readers
do not experience this as a structural fault — they experience it as the piece
being slow, and they leave.

**The one fix:** move the "most teams think" block to the top, ahead of the
router. Nothing needs rewriting. That is usually true of order failures, and it
is worth saying explicitly to an author bracing for a rewrite.

---

## Grading calibration

Two habits keep grades honest:

- **`weak` is not a compromise grade.** If you can quote a line that does the
  job, it is `present` even if the prose is dull. If you cannot, it is
  `missing`. `weak` is for the narrow case where a line does the job but a
  reader could plausibly read past it.
- **Structure is not quality.** A flat, joyless draft with four solid beats
  passes the structural check. Say so, and say separately that it is flat. The
  author is better served by two accurate judgements than one blended verdict.
